# Privacy policy sections

Use these sections as the baseline. Merge or reorder sections to fit the product. Do not drop a required disclosure: if there is no answer, use a placeholder. Jurisdiction files list the extra disclosures each law requires.

## 1. Who we are

- Legal entity name, registered address, and contact email for privacy.
- The role of the entity. For most products the business is the "controller" (EU/UK) or "business" (California) for account and usage data. For a B2B product that processes its customers' end-user data, the business is usually a "processor" or "service provider" for that data: say that the customer's own privacy policy governs it.
- DPO, EU representative, and UK representative contact details, if appointed.
- The products, sites, and apps the policy covers, and what it does not cover (third-party sites, customer data processed under a data processing agreement).

## 2. Personal data we collect

Use a table: category, examples, and source. Typical categories:

- **Account data**: name, email, password (stored as a hash), profile details.
- **Payment data**: billing name, address, and transaction records. State that the payment processor handles card details, unless the business stores them.
- **Content**: files, messages, prompts, and other content that users submit.
- **Usage data**: features used, pages viewed, actions, timestamps.
- **Device and technical data**: IP address, browser, operating system, device identifiers, approximate location from IP.
- **Communications**: support requests, feedback, survey answers.
- **Data from third parties**: OAuth providers (state which profile fields), payment processors, data from a user's employer for B2B accounts.

List sensitive data separately, if any, with the reason for its collection.

## 3. How we use personal data

List each purpose. Link each purpose to its data categories. Where the EU or UK GDPR applies, give the lawful basis for each purpose (see `eu-uk.md`). Typical purposes: provide the service, manage accounts, process payments, send transactional messages, send marketing (with consent or opt-out, as the law requires), security and fraud prevention, analytics and product improvement, legal compliance, and AI features.

## 4. AI features

Include this section if the product uses AI models on user data.

- Which content goes to which AI provider, and for which feature.
- Whether user content is used to train or improve models, by the business or by the provider. If it is not, say so only if the provider contracts or settings confirm it. If it is, say how users can opt out.
- Automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects: the logic involved, the significance, and how to get human review. See the jurisdiction files (GDPR Art. 22; Australia APP 1.7 to 1.9; California and Colorado ADMT and profiling rules).

## 5. How we share personal data

- Service providers (processors): name each one, or give categories with examples. Many businesses name each provider, which makes the policy easier to check. State the purpose and the data shared.
- Other recipients: other users (for shared or public content), professional advisers, acquirers in a merger or sale, and authorities when the law requires it.
- Sale and sharing: state clearly whether the business sells personal data or shares it for targeted advertising. Check this statement against the scan (advertising pixels, data enrichment tools).

## 6. International transfers

State where data is stored and processed (from hosting regions and provider locations). Give the transfer mechanism that each applicable law requires (see `eu-uk.md`, `australia.md`, `other-jurisdictions.md`).

## 7. Retention

Give a retention period, or the criteria that decide it, for each data category. Cover: active accounts, deleted accounts, backups, logs, payment records (tax and accounting laws often set minimum periods), and marketing consent records. Do not write a period that the code or operations do not follow.

## 8. Security

Describe reasonable measures in plain language: encryption in transit, encryption at rest (if true), access controls, and staff access limits. Do not promise absolute security. Do not list measures that the product does not have.

## 9. Your rights and choices

- List the rights that apply, grouped by jurisdiction where they differ.
- How to make a request: email, web form, or in-product settings. Identity verification. Authorized agents (US).
- Response times by law (for example, one month under the GDPR, 45 days under the CCPA).
- Marketing opt-out, and cookie choices.
- Right to complain to the supervisory authority, with the authority name and link where the law requires it.

## 10. Cookies and similar technologies

List the cookie categories, what each does, the main providers, and how to manage choices. For a long list, put it in a separate cookie policy and link to it. Describe how the product handles consent (EU/UK) and opt-out signals such as Global Privacy Control (US).

## 11. Children

State the minimum age. State whether the product is directed to children. Say what happens if the business learns that it collected a child's data without the required consent. If the product is directed to children, the jurisdiction rules (COPPA, GDPR Art. 8, UK Age Appropriate Design Code, Australia's Children's Online Privacy Code) need a lawyer.

## 12. Changes to this policy

How the business announces changes, how much notice it gives for material changes, and the "Last updated" date. California requires a review at least once every 12 months.

## 13. Contact us

Privacy contact email and postal address. Add jurisdiction-specific contacts (EU or UK representative, DPO, toll-free number if required).

## Short notices at the point of collection

When the user asks for a notice under a form (sign-up, newsletter, contact), write a short notice of about 50 to 150 words: the entity name, what the data is for, anything unexpected, any consent asked for, and a link to the full policy. Consent for marketing must be a separate, unticked choice where the EU, UK, or Australian rules apply.
