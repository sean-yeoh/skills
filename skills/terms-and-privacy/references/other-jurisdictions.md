# Other jurisdictions

Verified: 2026-11-22. This file is a starting point for each law: what usually triggers it and the disclosures it adds to a privacy policy. It is not complete. For each law that applies, read the primary source or the regulator's guidance before you draft its section. If you cannot check it, list the law in the draft notes as "not verified" and recommend local counsel.

For a global product, the EU GDPR structure usually covers most of these laws. Add a short section per country for the items that differ.

## Canada

- **PIPEDA** (federal private sector law) applies to commercial activity, except in provinces with substantially similar laws for in-province activity (Quebec, Alberta, British Columbia).
- Policy items: the purposes at or before collection, meaningful consent, the name or title of the person accountable for privacy, access and correction, how to complain, and the right to complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (priv.gc.ca).
- **Quebec Law 25** (Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, as amended): publish a confidentiality policy in clear language, name the person in charge of the protection of personal information and publish their title and contact details, disclose identification, location, and profiling technologies and how to turn them off, and disclose decisions made only by automated processing. Quebec users usually need French versions.
- Anti-spam: CASL needs consent for commercial electronic messages.

## Brazil (LGPD, Lei 13.709/2018)

- Applies to processing in Brazil, processing to offer goods or services to people in Brazil, or of data collected in Brazil.
- Policy items: purpose, form and duration of processing, controller identity and contact, shared data and purpose, the data subject rights (Art. 18), and the identity and contact of the data protection officer ("encarregado", Art. 41). Ten legal bases (Art. 7). International transfers need a mechanism (ANPD standard contractual clauses).
- Regulator: ANPD (gov.br/anpd). Portuguese versions are expected.

## India (Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023)

- **(time-sensitive)** The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified in November 2025 with phased start dates. Reported dates: consent manager rules from 13 November 2026; notice, consent, and most data fiduciary duties from 13 May 2027. Check the official gazette notification.
- Notices must be standalone and in clear language, itemize the personal data and purposes, explain how to withdraw consent and exercise rights, and explain how to complain to the Data Protection Board. Notices must be available in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution, on request.
- Verifiable parental consent for users under 18.

## Japan (APPI)

- Disclose the purpose of use. Third-party provision usually needs prior consent. Cross-border transfers need consent with information about the destination country's system, or an equivalent-standard recipient.
- Publish or make available: the business name and address, representative, purposes, the process for requests, and complaint contact. Regulator: Personal Information Protection Commission (ppc.go.jp).

## South Korea (PIPA)

- Strict, specific consent rules with separate consent for each purpose and for third-party provision. The privacy policy has prescribed contents, including the privacy officer. Regulator: PIPC (pipc.go.kr). Recommend local counsel for products that target Korea.

## Singapore (PDPA)

- Consent, purpose notification, access and correction, and a data protection officer whose business contact information is public. Breach notification to the PDPC within 3 calendar days of assessing a notifiable breach. Do Not Call rules for marketing messages. Regulator: PDPC (pdpc.gov.sg).

## China (PIPL)

- Separate consent for sensitive data, for disclosure to other handlers, and for cross-border transfers. Cross-border transfers need a security assessment, standard contract, or certification, depending on volume. Foreign handlers that target people in China must appoint a representative in China. Recommend local counsel for any product that targets users in China.

## New Zealand (Privacy Act 2020)

- Information Privacy Principles similar to the Australian APPs. Disclose overseas disclosures (IPP 12). Complaints go to the Privacy Commissioner (privacy.org.nz). From 1 May 2026, IPP 3A adds notice duties when collecting personal information indirectly **(time-sensitive)**.

## Switzerland (revised FADP, in force 1 September 2023)

- Close to the GDPR: identity and contact of the controller, purposes, recipients, countries of transfer and safeguards, and automated individual decisions. Regulator: FDPIC (edoeb.admin.ch).

## South Africa (POPIA)

- Notification under section 18 (similar to GDPR Art. 13), registration of the Information Officer with the Information Regulator, and a PAIA manual. Regulator: Information Regulator (inforegulator.org.za).
