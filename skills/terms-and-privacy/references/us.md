# United States

Verified: 2026-11-22. Check items marked **(time-sensitive)** against a primary source before you rely on them. US state privacy law changes every year.

Primary sources: California Civil Code §1798.100 et seq. and CPPA regulations (cppa.ca.gov), state attorney general sites, FTC rules and guidance (ftc.gov), eCFR for 16 CFR Part 312 (COPPA). For the list of state laws, use a maintained tracker (for example, the IAPP US State Privacy Legislation Tracker) and confirm each law on the state's own site.

## Baseline: a privacy policy is needed anyway

- **CalOPPA** (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §22575 to §22579): any commercial website or online service that collects personally identifiable information from California residents must post a privacy policy. It must state the categories of data collected, the categories of third parties it is shared with, how users can review and request changes (if offered), how the business notifies users of changes, the effective date, how the site responds to "Do Not Track" signals, and whether third parties can collect data about the user's activity across sites. There is no size threshold.
- **FTC Act Section 5**: the FTC treats a privacy policy that does not match real practice as deceptive. This is the main reason the policy must match the code.

## California (CCPA as amended by the CPRA)

**When it applies.** A for-profit business that does business in California, collects California residents' personal information, and meets one of these:

- annual gross revenue above the adjusted threshold. **(time-sensitive)** The CPPA set it at $26,625,000 from January 2025 and adjusts it in odd-numbered years. Check cppa.ca.gov.
- buys, sells, or shares the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households per year, or
- gets 50% or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing personal information.

If the business is below the thresholds, say so in the draft notes. Many small businesses still include a short California section, because the thresholds are easy to pass with advertising pixels.

**Privacy policy contents** (Civ. Code §1798.130 and 11 CCR §7011):

- Categories of personal information collected in the last 12 months, and categories of sensitive personal information.
- Categories of sources, and the business or commercial purposes for each category.
- Categories of third parties to which it discloses each category.
- Whether it sells or shares personal information (in the last 12 months), the categories sold or shared, and the categories of third parties.
- Whether it knowingly sells or shares the data of consumers under 16.
- Retention period for each category, or the criteria that decide it.
- Consumer rights: know (access), delete, correct, opt out of sale and sharing, limit use of sensitive personal information (if used beyond permitted purposes), and non-discrimination. How to submit requests (at least two methods, usually an email or web form and, for businesses that are not online-only, a toll-free number), how the business verifies requests, and how authorized agents can act.
- How the business responds to opt-out preference signals such as Global Privacy Control (GPC).
- Date of last update. Review and update at least once every 12 months.

**Sale and sharing.** "Share" means disclosure for cross-context behavioral advertising, with or without payment. Advertising pixels and retargeting tools usually count. If the business sells or shares, the site needs a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link (or an alternative opt-out link) and must honor GPC as an opt-out.

**Response times.** Confirm receipt within 10 business days. Respond within 45 calendar days. Extend by up to 45 more days with notice.

**(time-sensitive) New CPPA regulations.** Regulations on automated decision-making technology (ADMT), risk assessments, and cybersecurity audits were finalized in 2025 and took effect on 1 January 2026, with later compliance dates for some duties (ADMT notices from 1 January 2027). If the product uses ADMT for significant decisions, flag it for a lawyer and check the current regulations.

## Other state comprehensive privacy laws

**(time-sensitive)** About 20 states have comprehensive consumer privacy laws in effect as of late 2026, and more have enacted laws that start later (for example, in 2027). Thresholds, rights, and duties differ by state. Most share this baseline:

- Rights: access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out of targeted advertising, sale, and profiling for decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.
- Opt-in consent before processing sensitive data, in most states.
- An appeal process when the business refuses a request, with a way to contact the state attorney general.
- A 45-day response time, usually with one extension.
- Many states require businesses to honor universal opt-out signals such as GPC.

For a national product, write one US section that covers the shared rights, the appeal process, and sensitive data consent, then add state sections only where a state requires specific text. Check each state's current law in the tracker before you name it in the policy. Do not list states from memory.

## Children: COPPA (15 U.S.C. §6501 et seq.; 16 CFR Part 312)

- Applies to operators of websites and online services directed to children under 13, or with actual knowledge that they collect personal information from a child under 13.
- Requires a clear online notice, verifiable parental consent before collection, parental rights to review and delete, data minimization, and reasonable security.
- **(time-sensitive)** The FTC amended the COPPA Rule in 2025. Compliance with most amendments was required by 22 April 2026. Changes include separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's data to third parties (for example, for targeted advertising), a written data retention policy, and a written information security program.
- If the product is directed to children or is mixed-audience, stop and recommend a lawyer. This skill does not draft COPPA notices without that review.
- For a general-audience product, state that the service is not directed to children under 13 (or the higher minimum age), that the business does not knowingly collect their data, and how a parent can ask for deletion.

Several states also have laws on minors' data (for example, under 16 or under 18) and age-appropriate design codes. Check them if teenagers are likely users.

## Email and SMS

- **CAN-SPAM** (15 U.S.C. §7701 et seq.) for commercial email: accurate header and subject lines, a valid physical postal address, a clear opt-out that works for at least 30 days after sending, and opt-outs honored within 10 business days. Transactional emails are mostly exempt.
- **TCPA** (47 U.S.C. §227) for marketing texts and calls: prior express written consent for automated marketing texts. Flag SMS marketing for a lawyer.

## Automatic renewal and cancellation

- **(time-sensitive) California Automatic Renewal Law** (Bus. & Prof. Code §17600 to §17606), as amended by AB 2863 for contracts from 1 July 2025: clear and conspicuous terms before the purchase, express affirmative consent to the renewal terms, cancellation through the same medium the consumer used to sign up (online sign-up means online cancellation), notices before a free trial converts and before price changes, and an annual reminder. Many other states have similar laws.
- The FTC "Click-to-Cancel" amendments to the Negative Option Rule were vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025. ROSCA (15 U.S.C. §8401 to §8405) still applies to online negative option offers. Check for any new FTC rulemaking.

These are product duties. Put the needed flows in the publishing checklist.

## Terms: US-specific clauses

- **Arbitration and class action waiver**: generally enforceable under the Federal Arbitration Act if the user clearly agreed (clickwrap). Include an opt-out window (for example, 30 days), a small claims carve-out, and a mass-arbitration procedure if the user wants one. This is a business and legal choice: ask the user, and recommend a lawyer review.
- **Disclaimers**: put warranty disclaimers and liability limits in conspicuous text (often capitals). Add "some states do not allow the exclusion of certain warranties or limitation of liability, so some of these limits may not apply to you".
- **DMCA** (17 U.S.C. §512): to keep the safe harbor for user-uploaded content, the business needs a designated agent registered with the US Copyright Office (copyright.gov), a published notice-and-takedown process, a counter-notice process, and a repeat infringer policy. Add the agent registration to the publishing checklist.
- **Section 230** (47 U.S.C. §230) protects many platforms from liability for user content. It does not need text in the terms, but the terms should keep the right to remove content.

## Text you can adapt

```markdown
### Additional information for US residents

Depending on the state where you live, you may have the right to:

- know what personal information we collect and access it,
- correct inaccurate personal information,
- delete personal information,
- get a copy in a portable format, and
- opt out of targeted advertising, the sale of your personal information, and profiling for significant decisions.

We [do not sell or share / sell or share] personal information. [If applicable: To opt out, use the "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link, or turn on Global Privacy Control in your browser.]

To make a request, email [PRIVACY_EMAIL] or use [REQUEST_FORM_URL]. We will verify your identity before we act. An authorized agent can make a request for you. We respond within 45 days. If we refuse your request, you can appeal by replying to our decision. If your appeal is refused, you can contact your state attorney general.

We will not discriminate against you for using these rights.
```
