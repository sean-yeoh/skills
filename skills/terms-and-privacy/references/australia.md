# Australia

Verified: 2026-11-22. Check items marked **(time-sensitive)** against a primary source before you rely on them.

Primary sources: Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and Schedule 1 (Australian Privacy Principles) at legislation.gov.au, OAIC guidance (oaic.gov.au), Australian Consumer Law (Schedule 2, Competition and Consumer Act 2010), ACCC guidance (accc.gov.au), Spam Act 2003.

## When the Privacy Act applies

- Applies to Australian Government agencies and to private organisations with annual turnover above AUD 3 million.
- Also applies to some small businesses regardless of turnover: health service providers, businesses that trade in personal information, contractors to the Commonwealth, credit reporting bodies, and businesses that opt in.
- An overseas organisation is covered if it has an "Australian link", for example, it carries on business in Australia and collects or holds personal information in Australia.
- If the business is below the threshold and no exception applies, say so in the draft notes. Many small businesses still follow the APPs because customers and investors expect it.

## Privacy policy contents (APP 1.4)

The APP privacy policy must state:

- the kinds of personal information collected and held,
- how it is collected and held,
- the purposes of collection, holding, use, and disclosure,
- how a person can access their information and ask for correction,
- how a person can complain about a breach of the APPs, and how the business will deal with the complaint,
- whether the business is likely to disclose information to overseas recipients, and, if practicable, the countries where they are likely to be.

## Automated decisions (APP 1.7 to 1.9)

**(time-sensitive)** From 10 December 2026 (Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024), if the business uses a computer program to make, or substantially and directly help make, decisions that could significantly affect a person's rights or interests, and uses personal information to do so, the privacy policy must describe:

- the kinds of personal information used in the program,
- the kinds of decisions made solely by the program, and
- the kinds of decisions where the program substantially and directly helps a person decide.

Read the OAIC APP 1 guidelines and the APP 1.7 to 1.9 fact sheet for the current test before you draft this section.

## Other APP duties that affect the policy

- **APP 2**: let people deal anonymously or under a pseudonym where practicable.
- **APP 5**: notify at or before collection (identity, purposes, consequences if not collected, usual disclosures, overseas disclosures, access and complaints). A short notice at the sign-up form, with a link to the policy, often does this.
- **APP 7**: direct marketing needs a simple opt-out. Sensitive information needs consent for marketing.
- **APP 8**: before disclosing to an overseas recipient (including cloud providers outside Australia, unless they only store data and cannot access it), take reasonable steps so the recipient does not breach the APPs. The business usually stays accountable for the recipient. Disclose the likely countries.
- **APP 11**: reasonable security, and destroy or de-identify information that is no longer needed.
- **APP 12 and 13**: access and correction. Organisations respond within a reasonable period (OAIC guidance: usually 30 days).

## Notifiable Data Breaches scheme (Part IIIC)

Covered entities must notify affected individuals and the OAIC of an eligible data breach that is likely to result in serious harm. The policy can say that the business follows the scheme. Do not promise timelines that the business cannot meet.

## Complaints

The policy must explain how to complain to the business. If the person is not satisfied, they can complain to the OAIC (oaic.gov.au).

## Other changes from the 2024 amendments

**(time-sensitive)**

- A statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy started on 10 June 2025.
- The OAIC must develop a Children's Online Privacy Code by 10 December 2026. It will apply to online services likely to be accessed by children. Check its status and scope if children could use the product.
- Tiered civil penalties and infringement notices, including for some privacy policy defects.

Proposals from the Privacy Act Review that were not in the 2024 Act (for example, a general right to erasure, removal of the small business exemption, and a "fair and reasonable" test) are not law as of the verified date. Do not draft them as current law. Check for later reform bills.

## Australian Consumer Law in the terms

- Consumer guarantees for services (due care and skill, fitness for purpose, reasonable time) cannot be excluded, restricted, or modified (ACL s. 64). The terms must not say or imply otherwise. A blanket "no refunds" clause can be misleading.
- The guarantees apply to "consumers", which includes businesses that buy services that cost up to AUD 100,000, or that are of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic, or household use.
- For services not of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal use, a business may limit its liability for breach of a guarantee to supplying the services again or paying for that (ACL s. 64A).
- **Unfair contract terms**: since 9 November 2023, proposing or relying on an unfair term in a standard form consumer or small business contract can incur civil penalties. Common risks: one-sided rights to change terms or prices, broad indemnities, one-sided termination, and broad liability exclusions.

Text you can adapt:

```markdown
Nothing in these Terms excludes, restricts, or modifies any guarantee, right, or remedy you have under the Australian Consumer Law or any other law that cannot be excluded. Where the law lets us limit our liability for breach of such a guarantee, our liability is limited, at our option, to supplying the services again or paying the cost of having them supplied again.
```

## Spam Act 2003

Commercial electronic messages (email and SMS) need consent (express or inferred), must identify the sender, and must have a working unsubscribe. Honour unsubscribe requests within 5 working days.

## Text you can adapt

```markdown
### Privacy in Australia

We handle personal information in line with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles.

You can ask to access or correct the personal information we hold about you by emailing [PRIVACY_EMAIL]. We usually respond within 30 days.

If you have a complaint about how we handle your personal information, email [PRIVACY_EMAIL]. We will investigate and respond within [30] days. If you are not satisfied with our response, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner at oaic.gov.au.

We may disclose personal information to service providers in [COUNTRIES], such as our hosting provider in [COUNTRY].
```
